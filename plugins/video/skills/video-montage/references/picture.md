# Picture

## Pacing

- A visual change (cut, push, callout, new caption) every 2 to 4 seconds; faster in the first 5, slower at a reveal.
- One or two voice-free beats of 1.5 to 3 seconds in a minute, just before the reveal. A slow shot works only while a question is pending; otherwise it is dead air.
- Open on the question, in the biggest type of the film, over something moving. Drop any small line that adds nothing to the first second.
- A short loops: end on an image that cuts cleanly back to the first frame. Keep the end card short.

## Photos

- Use the highest resolution source. Upscale before cropping, and prefer stills over soft video.
- Push 1.0 to about 1.1 for a quiet photo, up to about 1.2 in the hook. Linear. Set `transformOrigin` on the subject.
- Move with `translate` and `scale`. Measured on a pan of a third of a pixel a frame: with `left`, 39 of 59 frame changes were identical frames; with `translate`, none.
- Check every edge at full size: filler blur, baked-in watermarks, scan borders. Scale up from a corner to crop them, with marks inside the same wrapper so they stay aligned.
- If a source has a logo or credit burned in, crop it and add the credit properly, including any licence the file needs.
- Prefer full-bleed. For a then-and-now, stack two full halves with the same subject in the same place.

## Shaky or low-resolution clips

Stabilise first, or use stills from the same shoot with a push. Shaky, soft footage next to clean graphics is the first thing audits flag.

## Callouts

- 3 to 5 in a minute, at most one a shot, one style.
- The strongest is a slow push while the surroundings dim gently on the same curve. Use it only when the subject is hard to find.
- A drawn ring: 5 to 8 px at 1080 wide, drawn over 18 to 24 frames, in stepped jumps (about 12 a second) so it feels hand-drawn against smooth footage.
- A target under about 5% of the frame width needs an arrow or a push, not just a ring.
- Colour must contrast with the picture. Add a dark outline under any light or gold mark.
- Move an arrow in, then let it bob a few pixels along its axis. Start the bob from zero so nothing jumps.
- Labels land on the spoken word and stay at least 1.5 seconds.

## Type

- One family, two weights. Tags at 38 px or more at 1080 wide; anything near 24 px is unreadable on a phone.
- Small labels over a photo sit on a solid pill, not a text shadow.
- Use a soft scrim behind a title zone only, not a dim over the whole photo: the real image is your best asset.
- Logo: one, light, no box, away from the platform's interface. Hide it where it would double up.

## One look

Normalise each source first, then one shared grade. Grain and dust belong only on archive material.

## Transitions

Hard cuts by default. Allow one or two motivated matches in a film, where the same shape or camera carries across the cut. Avoid light leaks, spins and stock wipes.

## Before and after, reconstruction, honesty

Label any reconstruction on screen as a reconstruction. Say what is known; do not invent detail. If a card and the voice differ on a word, make them match, or be ready to say why the card is the precise one.
